The 4 Questions Solved For Every Indian Region

Question 1

What is Changing?

Detects if monsoon rain is compressing into cloudbursts, ground temperatures are passing 45°C, or soil is desiccating.

Question 2

Where in India?

Pinpoints regional contrast: drying Indo-Gangetic basin vs wetting Western Coast; extreme Thar desert heat vs humid coastal heat.

Question 3

By How Much?

Calculates decadal rate using Sen's slope estimator compared against India's official 30-year IMD baseline normal (1991–2020).

Question 4

Is It Significant?

Verifies with >99% statistical confidence (Mann-Kendall test, p < 0.05, FDR controlled) that this is not a temporary 1-year fluke.

Scientific Detection Methodology & Statistical Framework
01
Non-Parametric Sen’s Slope Estimator

Calculates the median of all pairwise temporal slopes across 31 continuous years (1995–2025). Robust against extreme precipitation deluges, heatwave spikes, and non-Gaussian environmental distributions.

02
Mann-Kendall Rank Trend Significance (p < 0.05)

Quantifies whether observed directional trends are statistically significant non-random shifts ($p < 0.05$) versus temporary 1-year weather noise.

03
Coupled Multi-Sensor Triangulation

Cross-correlates thermal skin amplification (MODIS) against root-zone desiccation (SMAP) and rainfall anomalies (GPM) to uncover compound biogeophysical feedback loops.

04
Homogeneous Macro-Regional Zonation

Decomposes pan-India observations across 6 macro-regions: Indo-Gangetic Basin, Thar Desert, Central India Farmlands, Western Coast & Ghats, Northeast Hills, and South Deccan Plateau.

Core Purpose: Change is continuous across Earth’s interconnected environmental systems. While a single dry year is weather noise, multi-decadal shifts reflect systematic physical changes in monsoon wind currents, atmospheric vapor pressure deficits, and surface albedo.
